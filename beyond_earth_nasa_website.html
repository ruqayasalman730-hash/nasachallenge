Humanity's footprints beyond Earth

Since the 1960s, space missions have left scientific equipment on the Moon and Mars. Some devices completed their missions, while others still help scientists learn about our universe. Explore three important examples below.

01 / The Moon

Apollo 11

🌕
July 1969

Mission overview

Apollo 11 was the first crewed mission to land astronauts on the Moon. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin explored the lunar surface while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit.

The Apollo 11 mission returned the first samples of Moon rocks and soil collected by humans.
🛰️
Equipment

Experiments left behind

Apollo astronauts deployed scientific instruments on the Moon. These experiments helped researchers study the lunar environment and its interior.

02 / Mars

The Viking Program

🔴
1975–1976

Mission overview

NASA's Viking 1 and Viking 2 spacecraft carried orbiters and landers to Mars. The landers studied the planet's surface, atmosphere and soil.

Viking 1 became the first U.S. spacecraft to successfully land on Mars in July 1976.
🤖
Equipment

Viking landers

The landers used cameras, weather instruments and experiments to investigate the Martian environment and search for signs of biological activity.

03 / Moon science

Lunar Laser Ranging

✨
Since 1969

What is it?

Apollo astronauts placed special reflector panels on the Moon. Scientists send laser pulses from Earth toward the reflectors and measure the time it takes for the light to return.

The reflectors help scientists measure the Earth–Moon distance with remarkable precision.
🔭
Scientific purpose

Why does it matter?

These measurements help scientists study the Moon's orbit, test ideas about gravity and understand how the Earth–Moon distance changes over time.
